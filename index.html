IEEE TCAD 2026 · IEEE/ACM International Conference on Codesign of Embedded Systems (CODES) 2026

Bern2Edge: an end-to-end neurosymbolic compiler for edge deployment

Bern2Edge takes a large pretrained network and distills it into a compact Bernstein polynomial network, then deploys it on FPGAs through one of two paths. The first stores each neuron's activation as a lookup table with linear interpolation, so the cost stays fixed no matter the polynomial degree. The second extracts oblique symbolic rules from activation regimes, giving an interpretable model with a small fallback tree.

The framework covers feed-forward layers in MLPs and transformers and is synthesized on an AMD KV260 and a Xilinx Spartan-7.

+2.12 ppaccuracy over equivalent ReLU students
9×fewer rules than ECLAIRE, a state-of-the-art rule extraction method, with higher fidelity
−99.8%latency vs. W8A8 teacher, within 0.5 pp accuracy
−61.0%latency on TinyBERT4 at matching SST-2 accuracy

Exponential approximation rates and parameter efficiency of learnable Bernstein activations

Deep Bernstein Networks (DBNs), also called DeepBern-Nets, are neural networks that replace fixed activations such as ReLU with learnable Bernstein polynomials. Each neuron's activation is a weighted combination of Bernstein basis functions, so its shape is trained along with the weights. We prove that DBN approximation error decays as O(n−L) in polynomial degree n and depth L, exponentially faster than the polynomial rate of ReLU networks, while the networks stay differentiable.

Across 1,344 experiments on HIGGS and SUSY, DBNs match ReLU networks with far fewer parameters and train faster.

O(n−L)approximation error rate, exponential in depth
>70%fewer parameters in most architectures
26%of ReLU's training epochs to match its performance
−45%final loss vs. ReLU, at best

IEEE EPEC 2025 · Western University

Deep learning-driven dual-axis tilting solar panel system

A dual-axis solar tracker that replaces light-sensor arrays with deep learning. Neural networks trained on six years of solar position data predict the sun's elevation and azimuth from the date and time. An Arduino then drives two servo motors to tilt a monocrystalline panel on 3D-printed mounts. Dropping the sensors simplifies the circuit and lowers cost and operating power.

The elevation and azimuth models were tuned with random-search hyperparameter optimization. The prototype was then tested outdoors in London, Ontario against a fixed-angle panel.

+10.45%average voltage vs. a fixed panel over a 7.5-hour outdoor test (target 7.96%)

0.00907test MSE on normalized azimuth angle
